# Literature around Jegadeesh and Titman (1993)

Gathered by the literature_scout subagent on 2026-10-18. Publisher sites (ScienceDirect, Emerald, SAGE, SSRN) blocked full text, so most entries rest on abstracts. Numbers are quoted only where an abstract or a data page gave them. Items marked "not read" have only a bibliographic record.

## The authors' follow-ups

- **Jegadeesh, N. and Titman, S. (2001).** Profitability of Momentum Strategies: An Evaluation of Alternative Explanations. *Journal of Finance* 56(2), 699-720. doi:10.1111/0022-1082.00342; NBER WP 7159.
  - Momentum profits continued in the 1990s, out of sample, which argues against data snooping.
  - Returns reverse 4 to 5 years after formation, which they read as cautious support for behavioural "delayed overreaction".
  - They reject a pure explanation by cross-sectional dispersion in expected returns.
- **Jegadeesh, N., Luo, J., Subrahmanyam, A. et al. (2025).** Short-term reversals and longer-term momentum around the world. *Review of Financial Studies* 38(12), 3673. Not read.

## International evidence

- **Rouwenhorst, K. G. (1998).** International Momentum Strategies. *Journal of Finance*. doi:10.1111/0022-1082.95722.
  - Twelve European countries, 1980-1995.
  - An internationally diversified winner-minus-loser portfolio earns more than 1% a month after risk adjustment, and momentum appears in all twelve countries.

## Explanations and critiques

- **Daniel, K. and Moskowitz, T. J. (2016).** Momentum Crashes. *Journal of Financial Economics* 122(2), 221-247. NBER WP 20439.
  - Momentum crashes come in panic states, after market falls and when volatility is high, and coincide with rebounds.
  - Scaling exposure by forecast mean and variance roughly doubles the Sharpe ratio. This matches JT's own 1932 example (p. 85).
- **Wiest, T. (2023).** Momentum: what do we know 30 years after Jegadeesh and Titman's seminal paper? *Financial Markets and Portfolio Management*. doi:10.1007/s11408-022-00417-8. A survey. Not read.

## Costs and implementability

- **Lesmond, D., Schill, M. and Zhou, G. (2004).** The Illusory Nature of Momentum Profits. *Journal of Financial Economics*. Not read; the full text was blocked. Its title claim, that trading costs absorb momentum profits, is what this study's after-cost results will test for India.

## India

- **Sehgal, S. and Balakrishnan, I. (2002).** Contrarian and Momentum Strategies in the Indian Capital Market. *Vikalpa*. doi:10.1177/0256090920020103. Long-term reversal appears once short-term momentum is controlled for, and long-horizon contrarian strategies are profitable. Period, J/K and WML not retrieved.
- **Sehgal, S. and Ilango, B. (2009).** Momentum Profits, Portfolio Characteristics and Asset Pricing Models. The CAPM fails to explain Indian momentum; Fama-French three factors explain it partly.
- **Sehgal, S. and Jain, S. (2011).** Short-term Momentum Patterns in Stock and Sectoral Returns: Evidence from India. *Journal of Advances in Management Research* 8(1). Stock and sector momentum. Not read.
- **Ansari, V. and Khan, S. (2012).** Momentum Anomaly: Evidence from India. *Managerial Finance*. doi:10.1108/03074351211193730. JT portfolio construction on Indian shares; tests risk and behavioural explanations. Numbers not retrieved.
- **Agarwalla, S. K., Jacob, J. and Varma, J. R. (2013).** Four Factor Model in Indian Equities Market. IIMA WP 2013-09-05; SSRN doi:10.2139/SSRN.2334482.
  - Builds Indian Fama-French and momentum factors from CMIE Prowess, 1993-2013, with care over illiquid stocks and the mid-1990s "vanishing companies".
  - The live data library reports the WML factor at about 10.65% a year (geometric mean, January 1994 to December 2025).
- **Mohapatra, S. and Misra, A. K. (2020).** Momentum Returns: A Portfolio-Based Empirical Study ... in Indian Stock Market. *IIMB Management Review*. NSE stocks. Not read.

## Data sources found

- **IIMA Indian Fama-French-Momentum data library:** https://faculty.iima.ac.in/iffm/Indian-Fama-French-Momentum/
  - Free. October 1993 to December 2025, survivorship-bias-adjusted, NSE and BSE.
  - Publishes a WML factor that can serve as a cross-check for this study's WML series.
  - Whether it carries a risk-free column is not yet confirmed; to check at the Data stage.
- **NIFTY200 Momentum 30 and NIFTY500 Momentum 50 indices** (niftyindices.com): a practitioner benchmark for long-only momentum, scored on volatility-adjusted 6- and 12-month returns. The methodology PDFs are still to be read.
